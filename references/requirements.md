# UMKT 2024 Rule Matrix

Source: *Pedoman Karya Ilmiah Akhir Mahasiswa*, Universitas Muhammadiyah Kalimantan Timur, revised edition, 2024. This matrix deliberately records only rules useful for reviewing a student submission. Confirm programme-specific requirements when the guide permits alternatives.

## 1. Choose the correct route

| Submission | Required or allowed organization |
| --- | --- |
| Proposal | Cover; title page; approval page; Introduction (background, problem formulation, objectives, benefits, and hypothesis/assumptions where needed); Research Method; References. Literature should establish the problem and novelty in the background. |
| Final report / thesis / dissertation | Front matter, main matter, and end matter. The main matter may use Alternative 1 (Introduction, Method, Results/Analysis and Discussion, Closing), Alternative 2 (Introduction, Literature Review/Hypothesis Development, Method, Closing), or an approved programme-specific Alternative 3. |
| Publication manuscript | Title; author name and affiliation; Indonesian abstract and keywords; English abstract and keywords; Introduction; Method; Results and Discussion; Conclusion; optional acknowledgement; References. Use Arabic section numbering, not thesis-style chapters. |

## 2. Final-report front and end matter

- Front matter uses lower-case Roman page numbers. Include cover, title, supervisor approval, approval/validation, originality statement, abstracts (Indonesian and English for a skripsi), preface, contents, and lists of tables, figures, appendices, and terms when needed.
- Cover: full title; work type; student name and NIM; UMKT logo (5 cm diameter); programme, faculty, university, and examination month/year. Capital letters; balanced and symmetrical layout.
- Abstract/abstract: one paragraph, at most 250 words, single-spaced. Include background/problem, purpose, method, results/new finding, and implications; do not cite references or include tables/figures. Include 3–5 alphabetized keywords in the corresponding language. The English abstract is italicized.
- Preface: academic/financial acknowledgements relevant to completing the work; at most two pages; 1.5 spacing.
- End matter: references, appendices, and narrative CV. Include only important directly relevant appendices; do not append raw data merely by default. Number appendices with Arabic numbers and keep their order aligned with first mention in the text.

## 3. Content checks

- Background must establish importance, a specific problem/gap, an approach justified by literature, and the work's position/contribution.
- Problem formulation is a concise statement, preferably a question, with the scope and any hypothesis/initial proposition where appropriate.
- Objectives use measurable action verbs and must be answered by the conclusion; avoid vague verbs such as “know”, “see”, or “understand”.
- Method must make readiness and validity/accountability of the research steps clear: location/scope, materials/tools (including survey forms when relevant), and data collection, processing, and analysis steps. Abstract disciplines may adapt the detail while retaining readiness and validity.
- Results/discussion follow objectives and lead to conclusions. Present findings before interpretation and discussion when combined.
- Conclusions answer objectives or hypothesis tests; they are not a recap of results, should be unambiguous, and are written as effective paragraphs. Suggestions follow from findings and implications.
- Main text is limited to 10,000 words; front and end matter are excluded.

## 4. Presentation checks for final reports

- A4; all margins 2.5 cm; Times New Roman 11 pt; 1.15 line spacing for body text. Maps, tables, figures, photos, diagrams, and similar items may vary as needed.
- First paragraph line indent: 0.75 cm; justified body text. Avoid standalone bullet lists in narrative paragraphs; use full sentences and inline numbering such as (i), (ii), (iii).
- Chapters: Roman numerals without a full stop, centered; start each chapter, References, and Appendices on a new page. Chapter titles are uppercase, bold, no period. Subheadings are bold sentence case, no period or underline. Do not exceed three heading levels.
- Main and end matter use Arabic page numbers; put page numbers at the lower left.

## 5. Publication-manuscript details

- Maximum 16 words in the title; title is centered, bold, Times New Roman 12 pt. Use capital letters for the first word, except ordinary capitalization rules for conjunctions and abbreviations.
- List full author names, institutional affiliations, and the author contact email beneath the title.
- Its abstract requirements remain: Indonesian and English versions, each no more than 250 words; 3–5 alphabetized keywords; one paragraph, one column. The appendix template specifies 10 pt TNR, justified, single-spaced abstracts, while the narrative publication section points to the general presentation rules. Report any conflict as a guide ambiguity rather than manufacturing a rule.

## 6. Citations and references

- Use Harvard style. In-text citations use surname and four-digit year: `Surname (Year)` or `(Surname, Year)`. Direct quotations must include a page number, separated from the year with a colon and no space.
- Two or three authors: write all names; use “dan” in prose and `&` in parenthetical citation. Four or more authors: first surname plus `et al.`. Distinguish same-author, same-year works with `a`, `b`, etc.
- Online sources need author/organization, year or `n.d.`, access date, and a reference-list URL. Personal communication belongs in text only, not the reference list.
- Reference list: alphabetic Harvard style. Every in-text source must appear in References and every listed source must be cited in the text. Sources should be relevant, current, and credible; the guide favors recent work, normally within five years.
- Minimum cited sources: final report 15; skripsi 20; tesis 40; disertasi 50. A field may have a different accepted currency window; the guide explicitly notes five years for fast-moving technology/management fields and up to ten years for general fields.

## 7. Tables and figures

- Every table/figure must be referred to in the text before it appears, be near that reference, and use sequential numbering by appearance. Do not include unreferenced illustrations.
- Tables: centered; title above; sentence case; no period; “Table” and its number bold. Tables longer than one page normally belong in appendices unless necessary for discussion; repeat the header if continued. Use 1.0 spacing in cells; TNR may be reduced to 8 pt. Notes and secondary-data sources go beneath the table in TNR 9 pt.
- Figures: centered on their own paragraph without added frames; title below; sentence case; no period; “Figure” and its number bold. Captions use TNR 11 pt. Ensure readable size, resolution, contrast, labels, and source/permission when applicable.

## 8. Language, integrity, and AI

- Use standard Indonesian, effective and complete sentences, and neutral academic language. Check capitalization, italics, compound words, prepositions, abbreviations, numbers, units, and punctuation against PUEBI/KBBI where the review has sufficient context.
- The guide defines fabrication, falsification, plagiarism, invalid authorship, conflicts of interest, and duplicate submission as academic-integrity violations. A document review cannot establish these matters; flag only concrete concerns for human review.
- AI may support feedback, language/structure improvement, exploration, and source discovery. The student must reconstruct output in their own original writing, cite the real relevant sources, compare AI-derived material with reputable sources, read the cited scholarship, and must not copy AI text directly or use AI as a substitute for research and authorship.
