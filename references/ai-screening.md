# AI-Writing and Hallucination Screening

Use this reference only when the requester asks for AI-writing screening, hallucination detection, citation verification, or a comparable integrity review.

## What this screen can and cannot establish

Do not infer that text was AI-written from polish, generic phrasing, fluency, unusual punctuation, repeated transitions, low variation in sentence length, or a detector score. These are non-diagnostic characteristics shared by many human-written and edited documents.

The review can instead identify verifiable problems and document-specific indicators that merit a conversation, correction, or formal human review. It cannot prove who authored text, whether a student used an AI tool, or whether an integrity violation occurred.

## Evidence-first review

Prioritize checks that can be traced to the document and, when available and permitted, an authoritative cited source:

1. **Citation existence and metadata.** Check for incomplete references, implausible combinations of author/title/journal/year/volume/pages, broken identifiers or URLs, duplicate entries, and cited works missing from the reference list.
2. **Citation entailment.** For a limited, documented sample of consequential claims, read the cited primary/reputable source and determine whether it supports the specific claim. Mark the result as supported, partially supported, contradicted, unverifiable, or source inaccessible.
3. **Factual consistency.** Compare claims with the submission's own tables, figures, method, sample, results, and conclusion. Flag internal contradictions, impossible chronology, mismatched units/denominators, and claims that exceed the reported evidence.
4. **Research traceability.** Check whether methods, data provenance, instruments, analysis steps, and reported results provide enough detail to follow the stated research process. Missing traceability is a content issue, not evidence of AI use.

When verifying sources externally, prefer the publisher, DOI registry, government/statistical authority, or primary research source. State the verification scope: which claims and sources were actually checked. Do not claim that unreviewed sources are valid.

## Risk indicators to report carefully

Report an indicator only when it is specific and locatable. Examples include:

- a citation that cannot be found with the supplied bibliographic details;
- a cited article whose title, authors, journal, year, or DOI conflict with a reputable record;
- a quotation that cannot be located in its cited source or lacks the required page number;
- a numerical, historical, legal, or scientific assertion contradicted by a cited authoritative source;
- a claim presented as a result but unsupported by the document's method, data, tables, or analysis;
- generic references to studies, standards, or statistics without a traceable source where one is necessary.

Avoid labels such as “AI-generated,” “hallucinated,” or “fake” for an entire document. Use precise findings instead, such as “citation metadata could not be verified” or “the cited source does not support the stated claim.”

## Reporting template

For each screened item, report:

| Field | Include |
| --- | --- |
| Location | Page, section, paragraph, table, or figure |
| Claim or citation | A short, necessary excerpt or bibliographic identifier |
| Check performed | Metadata, entailment, internal consistency, or traceability |
| Evidence | Source record or conflicting material, or why it could not be checked |
| Finding | Supported, partially supported, contradicted, unverifiable, or inaccessible |
| Next action | Correct citation, qualify claim, supply evidence, or refer for human review |

Label unverified items as **Needs human verification** unless there is direct evidence of a factual or citation error. Keep scope proportional: a small representative sample is useful for routine review; expand only when the requester asks or the preliminary screen finds repeated substantive issues.

## Relation to the UMKT guide

The 2024 guide permits AI as a support tool but requires students to reconstruct output in their own writing, cite relevant sources, compare AI-derived material with reputable sources, read the referenced scholarship, and retain responsibility for the research and writing. This screen supports those expectations through verification; it does not replace an institutional academic-integrity investigation.
